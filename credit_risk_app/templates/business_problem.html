Why Serious Delinquency Prediction Matters

Across {{ "{:,}".format(summary.row_count) }} account-months, {{ summary.kpis.roll_90p_6m_rate }}% eventually roll into 90+ DPD within six months. This page explains the vocabulary and the business case before the data itself takes over on later pages.

What is DPD?

Days Past Due — how many days an account has gone without a required payment being made. It resets toward zero whenever the account catches up, and climbs whenever a scheduled payment is missed.

What is delinquency?

An account is delinquent once it has any amount past due — DPD > 0. Delinquency is a spectrum, not a single event: early delinquency (a few days late) is common and often self-resolves; serious delinquency (90+ DPD) rarely does.

What does "roll" mean?

An account rolls when it moves from one delinquency stage to a worse one — Current to 30+ DPD, or 30+ to 90+ DPD. roll_to_90p_6m asks a forward-looking question: starting from wherever the account is today, does it roll all the way to 90+ DPD within the next six months?

Why 90+ DPD specifically?

90+ DPD is the industry-standard threshold for serious delinquency — the point where recovery becomes materially harder, provisioning requirements typically kick in, and an account is functionally in or near default. It is a meaningful line, not an arbitrary one.

Why predict this early, and what does a bank do with it?

Reactive collections (without a model)

Collections acts only after an account is already 30+ or 60+ DPD. By then the borrower may already be in serious financial stress, contact rates drop, and recovery costs rise. The bank is always one step behind the account's own trajectory.

Proactive early warning (with this model)

Accounts still Current or only mildly delinquent, but showing deteriorating behavior — rising utilization, thinning payment ratios, recent bounces — get flagged before the roll happens. That opens the door to softer, cheaper interventions: a payment reminder, a restructuring offer, a credit-line adjustment.
